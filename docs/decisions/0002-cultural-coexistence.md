# 0002 — Cultural coexistence, contested history and strategic agency
Status: Proposed
Date: 2026-11-28

## Context
Builds on [0001](0001-creative-direction.md). The founding collaborator endorsed a shift away from a linear rise-and-fall story and a technology-versus-primitivism binary toward contested territory, native inhabitants and factional interests.

## Decision proposed
Focus on the mystery within the Lyth rather than extensive external-world lore. Treat regions as inhabited homes with overlapping claims, material needs and histories. Explore colonial dispossession and coexistence without making species synonymous with political allegiance or moral character.

> The player arrives believing they are helping their people reclaim a lost world. They gradually discover that the world was never lost to those who continued living in it.
>
> Your ethical choices are expressed through your strategic decisions.
>
> Every region is somebody's home before it is the player's battlefield.

## Rationale
The campaign can challenge initial allegiances through discovery and tactical choices, making ecological restoration inseparable from the interests of inhabitants.

## Implications
Faction-issued objectives may be partial or blunt; discoverable alternatives and secondary objectives can produce different consequences. Technology may help or harm depending on use, access and power. Coexistence means integration without forced cultural assimilation. Preserve asymmetries of power and legitimate grievances rather than assuming every claim is equally justified.

## Alternatives and open questions
The earlier five-age timeline and technological-collapse hypothesis remain exploratory, not canon. Human protagonist, amnesia, first human settlement, faction roster, history and mission mechanics remain undecided. Team acceptance pending. No changes to competitive gameplay or roadmap.
