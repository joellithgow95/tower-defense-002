# Drowned Woods

A flooded woodland in the Lyth, and the setting of the first themed map. Nothing here is canon.

**Where each idea came from:**
- **Existing docs:** the short entry in [regions](../regions.md), from the second worldbuilding discussion; the creature concepts and level constraints in the [founding discussion](../ideas/founding-discussion.md); and the [Sluice at Alderfen](../../gameplay/campaign-missions.md) mission sketch.
- **Joel (2026-11-28):** the map's features. A sluice that is the only crossing, calling stones, spawning grounds, drowned trees, rising water, mist, and a spawning run between habitats. They're described as gameplay in [map events](../../gameplay/map-events.md).
- **Agent suggestions:** everything else on this page, marked *(suggestion)*. These are possibilities to react to, not proposals anyone has endorsed.

## Landscape
From the docs: flooded woodland over submerged human roads and fields. The [geography](../geography.md) palette fits it closely: peat, moss, reeds, mist, raised ground and causeways.

From Joel's map:
- a deep channel splits the wood;
- one old sluice spans it;
- a stone causeway runs through the sluice;
- dead trees stand in the water;
- the water is still rising.

*(suggestion)* The drowned trees are an old wood of a single kind, killed when the water rose, standing grey and barkless. The living wood grows on peat banks and hummocks between them. Old field walls and road kerbs show as straight lines under the shallows, a sign that this was once dry and farmed.

## Inhabitants
From the docs: amphibious settlements whose homes, spawning grounds and trade routes depend on the water as it is now. There are also human communities whose claim to the drowned farmland is ancestral.

*(suggestion)* Who these people are is deliberately left open. Following [decision 0002](../../decisions/0002-cultural-coexistence.md), they are communities, not species, and they disagree among themselves. Freshwater merfolk ([civilizations](../civilizations.md)) are one candidate for some of the amphibious settlements, but nothing should assume it.

Wildlife: the spawning grounds belong to creatures the map treats as neutral ([issue #19](https://github.com/joellithgow95/tower-defense-002/issues/19)). Mireback and Reedling, from the founding discussion, are the most natural fit, but none has been chosen.

## Ordinary day *(suggestion)*
- Boats move along the channel at dawn, before the mist lifts.
- Peat is cut from the high banks and stacked to dry.
- Eel and fish traps are checked at the sluice gates.
- Children are taught which shallows are safe to wade and which hide old ditches.
- Through the spawning season, households keep to set paths so the runs aren't disturbed.

## Material culture *(suggestion)*
- Stilt houses and reed-thatched roofs; boardwalks that can be lifted and moved as the water changes.
- Flat-bottomed boats and punt poles.
- Woven willow traps, and lanterns hung on poles to mark safe ways in the mist.
- The sluice and the causeway are older than anyone living. Who built them, and whether the sluice was meant to drain the wood or flood it, stays an open mystery.

## Resources *(suggestion)*
Peat for fuel, reeds for building, fish and eels, willow, and the fertile silt the flood leaves behind. The farmland under the water is a resource only to those who want it drained.

## Overlapping claims
From the docs: human claims to ancestral farmland conflict with the homes that exist now. The Alderfen sketch makes the sluice the hinge. Opening it reclaims fields, but costs habitat and homes.

*(suggestion)* The channel is also a trade route. Whoever controls the sluice controls who can cross dry-shod, and who goes by boat.

## Internal disagreements *(suggestion)*
- Among the amphibious communities: some would accept a partly opened sluice in return for help with the rising water; others distrust any concession (the "wetland elder" character sketch in [contested Lyth](../ideas/contested-lyth.md)).
- Among the human communities: some want the fields back; others, like the "naturalist" sketch, argue for learning to live with the water.

## Ecological stress
From the docs: rising water may genuinely threaten everyone.

*(suggestion)* The pressure works in both directions:
- Draining dries the spawning grounds.
- Unchecked rising water drowns the living wood as it once drowned the old one, and pushes settlements onto ever smaller banks.

Why the water is rising is left open.

- *(suggestion)* Escort a spawning run across the causeway while both sides want the crossing.
- *(suggestion)* Find what the calling stones were for before using them in a fight.

## Open questions
- Is "Drowned Woods" the region's name, a place within it, or a working title? Alderfen is named in the mission sketch, but how it relates to the Drowned Woods isn't settled.
- Who built the sluice and the causeway, and what were they for? (Keep open; don't answer in passing.)
- Why is the water rising?
- What are the calling stones, and who used them?
- Which creatures spawn here, and what do they do for the wetland?
