# Strategic agency
Faction briefings present objectives from a particular perspective. Battlefield discoveries reveal competing interests and alternative resolutions. Ethical choices should be expressed through tower placement, resource allocation, protection, routing and other strategic actions, not only dialogue.
## Example: The Sluice at Alderfen
Human settlement requests securing an ancient sluice to reclaim farmland. Discover amphibious defenders protecting spawning grounds and a damaged secondary channel.
| Approach | Tactical objective | Possible consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reclaim | Secure and fully open primary sluice | Farmland recovered; habitat and homes lost |
| Preserve | Protect spawning grounds and stop drainage | Wetland remains viable; food shortage unresolved |
| Reconcile | Repair and defend secondary channel while protecting communities | Shared water access, additional effort and compromise |
These are illustrative, not a universal good/bad choice or finalized balance. Alternative solutions need not always be harder or available.
## Mechanics to explore
Multiple simultaneous defense sites; repair costs competing with towers; migration corridors; protection of formerly hostile creatures; optional waves and secondary objectives; map discoveries that reveal relationships. Existing maze routing may make placement consequential.
## Persistent consequences
Faction and individual relationships, political influence, resource access and ecological conditions could change subsequent missions. Avoid a single morality meter. Numerical reputation examples from discussion were illustrative only.
